# Patterns and repairs
Review signals, never authorship tests. Examples are original synthetic editorial fixtures; their facts are stipulated for the example. Use the smallest repair allowed by the selected mode.

| Pattern: notice | Why it weakens prose | When legitimate | Repair | Original example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thin argument: conclusion repeats premise | Gives no reason to agree or act | A brief reminder may need no argument | Connect supplied evidence to the point; flag missing support | “Planning helps because planning matters.” With supplied missed-deadline context: “Put each deadline beside its client so you can see which commitments overlap.” |
| Inflated significance: large outcome from small fact | Overstates what the evidence supports | Documented major effects or clearly subjective praise | Retain supported function; flag unsubstantiated outcomes | “This form revolutionizes hiring.” → “This form collects interview availability.” |
| Stock vocabulary: abstract verbs hide the action | Reader cannot tell what happens | Technical terms and intentional brand language | Name the actual action without synonym churn | “Leverage the sheet to facilitate alignment.” → “Record the agreed dates in the sheet.” |
| Repeated syntax: identical contrasts or triplets | Creates rhythm without adding distinctions | Real contrasts, memorable rhetoric, parallel procedures | Remove padding; preserve useful relationships | “Not just a calendar, but a compass.” → “The calendar shows the next deadline.” |
| Formulaic framing: generic intro or recap | Delays or repeats the useful information | Executive summaries, teaching signposts, necessary transitions | Start at the relevant point; end when the purpose is fulfilled | “In an ever-changing world, backups matter.” → “Back up the folder before editing it.” |
| Rhythm: uniform sentences or artificial fragments | Monotony or manufactured drama obscures logic | Deliberate dialogue, emphasis, stable procedural style | Join related fragments or split overloaded syntax; no variation quotas | “The file failed. Again. No warning.” → “The file failed again without warning.” Keep the original if it serves the narrator. |
| Formatting: decoration or unsuitable markup | Makes copy harder to use in its destination | Lists for steps, tables for comparison, meaningful headings | Choose structure by reader task, not suspicion of AI | “**Reminder:** **Send** the **invoice**.” → “Send the invoice.” |
| Generic emotion: announced feeling without basis | Sounds interchangeable or assigns invented feelings | Genuine supplied emotion, ceremonial language, fiction | Use supplied action/detail or restrained acknowledgement | “I deeply understand your devastating frustration.” → “The replacement arrived damaged too. I’m sorry.” Only use that detail if supplied. |
| Citations and residue: ghost sources or chat wrappers | Misrepresents evidence or exposes unfinished copy | A quote about chatbot language; real citations | Preserve valid citations; flag unresolved references; remove wrappers outside quotes | “Certainly! Experts prove it works.” → No publishable claim until the evidence or a narrower brief is supplied. |
| Certainty: stacked hedges or unjustified confidence | Hides the actual strength of evidence | Necessary scientific, legal, or factual qualification | Preserve scope; remove only redundant caution; never equate sequence with cause | “It could possibly perhaps help.” → “It may help.” A baseless claim still needs evidence. |

Keep exact quotes, locked strings, valid technical meanings, and required keywords. Do not replace “robust estimator” with “strong estimator.” Do not remove an Oxford comma, dash, passive construction, list, or CTA merely because a source calls it an AI tell. If repairing substance exceeds the mode, flag the issue separately.
